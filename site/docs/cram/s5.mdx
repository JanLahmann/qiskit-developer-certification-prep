## Set sampler primitive options such as resilience levels

### The SamplerOptions tree — and the one option that is not in it

Start with the trap the objective's own wording sets. The exam blueprint says "set sampler primitive options such as resilience levels", but in `qiskit-ibm-runtime` **SamplerV2 has no `resilience_level`** — that knob belongs to EstimatorV2, where it drives expectation-value mitigation (TREX at level 1, ZNE at level 2). `sampler.options.resilience_level = 1` does not silently do nothing; the options object is a validated pydantic dataclass, so it raises a `ValidationError` ("Object has no attribute 'resilience_level'"), and passing it through the constructor's `options={...}` mapping is rejected too.

What SamplerOptions actually holds, top level: `max_execution_time`, `environment`, `simulator`, `default_shots`, `dynamical_decoupling`, `execution`, `twirling`, `experimental`. The nested groups are where questions probe. `dynamical_decoupling` = `enable`, `sequence_type` (`XX` default, `XpXm`, `XY4`), `extra_slack_distribution`, `scheduling_method`, `skip_reset_qubits`. `twirling` = `enable_gates`, `enable_measure`, `num_randomizations`, `shots_per_randomization`, `strategy`. `execution` = `init_qubits`, `rep_delay`, `meas_type` — note there is no `shots` and no `seed_simulator` in there; the simulator seed lives in `simulator` (`noise_model`, `seed_simulator`, `coupling_map`, `basis_gates`). Anything misspelled is rejected outright: `experimental` is an opt-in dict you fill yourself, not a catch-all for unknown names.

Three ways to set them, all valid: an options mapping or `SamplerOptions` object in the constructor, per-attribute assignment afterwards (`sampler.options.dynamical_decoupling.enable = True`), or a bulk `sampler.options.update(default_shots=1024, dynamical_decoupling={"sequence_type": "XpXm"})`. "The options object is read-only" is false. What does NOT exist is the V1 habit `sampler.set_options(...)` — that is an `AttributeError`. Options you never touch stay `Unset` and take the server default, so your code's behaviour does not drift with the client version.

Finally, shots. The Sampler has a documented precedence chain, and it is a favourite question: a shot count carried in the PUB's third slot wins; then the `shots=` keyword on `run()`; then, if twirling is on, the product of `num_randomizations` and `shots_per_randomization`; then `options.default_shots`. Two corollaries: setting `default_shots = 1000` and then submitting `(isa, None, 50)` really does run 50 shots, and `shots` must be an integer — a one-element list raises `TypeError`. Beware also that the shot *default* is environment-dependent: a local fake backend falls through to 1024, while the Runtime service's own default is 4096.

- `sampler.options.resilience_level = 1` raises a pydantic `ValidationError` ("Object has no attribute 'resilience_level'") — resilience levels are an EstimatorV2 option, despite the objective's wording. — ⚙️ proven in [`s5-q014`](/docs/sections/s5)

## Bypass runtime error mitigations and implement your own

### Raw counts are a feature, not an omission

The Sampler is deliberately the low-level primitive: it hands back the sampled bitstrings, shot by shot, with no readout correction applied. That is the whole basis of this objective — you do not "bypass" Sampler mitigation by turning something off, because there is nothing on. The only noise-management options Sampler supports are **error suppression and twirling**: dynamical decoupling (pulse sequences that cancel coherent errors on idling qubits) and Pauli twirling of gates and/or measurements (randomizing over an ensemble of equivalent circuits so that arbitrary noise becomes Pauli noise). Neither rewrites your counts into "mitigated" values.

Everything with "mitigation" in its name — TREX measurement-error mitigation, zero-noise extrapolation, probabilistic error cancellation and amplification — is an Estimator resilience option, reached through `options.resilience.*` or the `resilience_level` shortcut. Those techniques correct *expectation values*, which is exactly why they have no Sampler equivalent: there is no single number to rescale, only a histogram.

So roll your own, in post-processing, from the data you already have. The two canonical schemes are worth being able to tell apart. **Post-selection** exploits a known symmetry of the ideal output: a Bell circuit can only produce `00` or `11`, so you drop the `01`/`10` shots that readout error sprinkled in and renormalize over what survives — on a real run that is discarding a couple of hundred shots out of four thousand. It is cheap and requires no calibration, but it only works when the ideal support is known and it throws information away. **Assignment-matrix (confusion-matrix) inversion** is the rigorous version: prepare each computational basis state with calibration circuits, record what you actually read back, assemble the matrix that maps true outcomes to observed ones, and invert it (or least-squares invert it) to correct your histogram. That is what packages such as M3/mthree automate, and the Sampler's raw per-shot record is precisely the input they need.

Practically, do the work on the arrays rather than the dictionaries. A `BitArray` supports boolean-mask post-selection on another register, `slice_bits`, `slice_shots`, `expectation_values` for diagonal observables, and `concatenate_bits` to merge registers — all faster than round-tripping through `get_counts()`. And remember what `get_bitstrings()` gives you: one entry per shot, in the order the shots were taken, which is the only view that lets you correlate outcomes across registers before aggregating.

**Key facts:**

- Sampler's entire noise-management surface is two techniques: dynamical decoupling and Pauli twirling. There is no readout-mitigation option to switch on or off. — 📖 [sampler-noise-management](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/guides/sampler-noise-management)
- TREX (measurement-error mitigation), ZNE and PEC are Estimator resilience options reached via `options.resilience.*` or `resilience_level` — they correct expectation values, so they have no Sampler counterpart. — 📖 [error-mitigation-and-suppression-techniques](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/guides/error-mitigation-and-suppression-techniques)
- Attempting to buy mitigation through an option fails loudly rather than silently: `sampler.options.resilience_level = 1` is a `ValidationError`, whether assigned or passed in the constructor's options dict. — ⚙️ proven in [`s5-q014`](/docs/sections/s5)
- Post-selection is something you code: a Bell run's raw counts `{'00': 1941, '11': 1822, '01': 90, '10': 147}` become `{'00': 1941, '11': 1822}` by dropping the odd-parity shots and renormalizing. — ⚙️ proven in [`s5-q028`](/docs/sections/s5)
- `get_bitstrings()` returns one entry per shot in the order taken (777 shots → a list of length 777) — the un-aggregated record an assignment-matrix or M3-style correction needs as input. — ⚙️ proven in [`s5-q029`](/docs/sections/s5)
- Do custom post-processing on the `BitArray`, not on counts dicts: it supports boolean-mask post-selection, `slice_bits`, `slice_shots`, `expectation_values` and `concatenate_bits`. — 📖 [primitive-input-output](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/guides/primitive-input-output)

## Understand the theoretical background behind the sampler primitive

### PUBs, broadcasting, and what actually comes back

Conceptually the Sampler does one thing: execute a circuit many times and record the measured register each shot — draws from the distribution the circuit defines over computational-basis states. It never returns amplitudes or expectation values; probabilities are frequencies you compute yourself, and their statistical error shrinks like 1/√N_shots, so halving the error costs four times the shots. The primitives interface supersedes `backend.run`, with Sampler providing the same direct hardware access.

Input is a **PUB** — a Primitive Unified Bloc. A Sampler PUB is a tuple of at most three entries: `(circuit, parameter_values, shots)`, the last two optional, and `run()` takes a *list* of them. The circuit must contain measurements. A bare circuit is an acceptable PUB-like element as long as it is inside the list: `run([isa])`, never `run(isa)`. Get the slots wrong and the failure is specific — `(isa, 256, vals)` gives `TypeError: shots must be an integer`, a four-element tuple gives `ValueError: The length of pub must be 1, 2 or 3`.

Parameter binding follows NumPy broadcasting with one rule bolted on: the LAST axis of the parameter array is reserved for the circuit's free parameters and is consumed during binding; whatever shape precedes it becomes the result's shape. Two parameters bound with a (6, 2) array give `data.meas.shape == (6,)`; a (3, 4, 2) array gives `(3, 4)`. If the trailing axis does not match the parameter count you get `ValueError: Length of ('p[0]', 'p[1]') inconsistent with last dimension` — and transposing, flattening or splitting into one PUB per row all raise the same thing; reshaping to (5, 2) is the fix. Shots are per parameter set, not per PUB: six sets at `shots=200` is 1200 circuit executions.

Output is a `PrimitiveResult` whose length is the number of PUBs you submitted — three circuits in, `len(result) == 3`, in order. Each `SamplerPubResult` has a `DataBin` holding one `BitArray` **per classical register, named after the register**: `measure_all()` creates a register called `meas`, `QuantumCircuit(2, 2)` creates one called `c`, and explicit registers keep their own names (`data.alice`, `data.bob`). Reading `data.meas` on a circuit that never used `measure_all` is the classic `AttributeError`. From a `BitArray`, `get_counts()` is the histogram, `get_int_counts()` the same histogram with integer keys (`'11'` → `3`), and `get_bitstrings()` the raw per-shot list. On a broadcast result, `get_counts()` with no index POOLS every parameter set (4 × 500 = 2000) while `get_counts(0)` gives one set's 500. And every bitstring is little-endian: qubit 0 is the rightmost character, `b[-1]`.

- The two primitives split the job: Sampler samples the output register of a circuit, Estimator computes expectation values of observables — and the primitives interface supersedes `backend.run`. — 📖 [primitives](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/guides/primitives)
